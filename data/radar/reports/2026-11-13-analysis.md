## 4. Innovation opportunities

- **Rule hypotheses:** all carry verdicts; no new rule fired.
- *(mine, carried)* **Plugin trust for agent harnesses.** The jailbreak
  plugin that grew to 550 stars in two days has now been deleted. Plugin
  markets clean themselves up after the fact, which is the gap a vetting
  layer would close up front.
- *(mine, new)* **Like and star provenance for model hubs.** The radar
  has now seen bulk-withdrawn likes on Hugging Face and many 50%-fork
  repos on GitHub. A service that scores how trustworthy a model's or
  repo's popularity is would serve anyone choosing what to install.
  - **Who might be there:** Hugging Face itself (it evidently removes
    likes) and repo-security vendors.
  - **What would have to be true:** buyers act on popularity signals, and
    the platforms don't expose their own fraud scores. Not researched yet.

## 5. Radar self-critique

- **Improvement made today: no more digit fragments as search terms.**
  - The shared tokenizer keeps letters and hyphens only, so "3d-models"
    gave "d-models" and "deepseek-v4" gave "deepseek-v".
  - Mining now keeps a term only if it appears as a word of its own in the
    group's text.
  - The fix lives on the radar side; the shared `sciscout` tokenizer and
    its tests are untouched.
  - The two fragments already on the watch list moved to the drop list.
  - A test pins it, using the real tokenizer's output.
- **Mining still picks weak terms:**
  - "mini" and "persistent" are generic.
  - "gentleman" is a piece of an owner's name (Gentleman-Programming). The
    owner filter matches whole owner names only.
  - These are next in the backlog.
- **The "standalone" and "secure" terms are still paying out spam days
  after being dropped.** Repos they registered stay in the registry and
  surface in the recheck list. Removing them is manual, one at a time:
  three today.
