# Physical bottlenecks

_Generated by `python -m nexus.cli bottleneck`._

How much of sciscout's commercialisation case can be moved from assumption to measurement with the data in this repository. The answer is: one input, for one sector.

## What was measured

- **Data:** Share of electricity generated by solar and by wind, by country and year. Our World in Data, energy dataset (owid/energy-data on GitHub); retrieved 2026-11-28 from <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/owid/energy-data/master/owid-energy-data.csv>. 424 country series, to 2025.
- **Assumed:** a tenfold rise in share of national electricity, from 1% to 10%, stands for the ramp from first commercial sales to peak share. The band is a choice; the report shows two others beside it.
- **Assumed:** grids generating under 10 TWh a year are left out: one plant can move a small grid's share by several points in a year, which measures the size of the grid rather than the pace of deployment.
- **Assumed:** solar and wind deployment stand in for energy hardware generally. A new chemistry or reactor may ramp slower (no supply chain yet) or faster (drop-in to existing lines); nothing here measures which.

## Back-test

The curve is refitted on data up to a cutoff year. Each ramp that had started but not finished by then gets a probability of finishing by 2025 and a 50% interval (conditional quartiles) for its total length; both are scored against what happened. A Brier score below the base-rate column means the curve tells unfinished ramps apart better than one shared guess (the base rate is computed from the outcomes, so it is not a forecast anyone could have made).

| Fitted to | Ramps (completed) | Unfinished at cutoff | Finished since: predicted | actual | Brier | Brier, base rate | 50% interval covered | Finished before interval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2012 | 42 (5) | 37 | 19.0 | 25 | 0.290 | 0.219 | 0 of 0 | 0 |
| 2015 | 64 (10) | 54 | 35.1 | 31 | 0.286 | 0.245 | 21 of 38 | 7 |
| 2018 | 86 (14) | 72 | 27.3 | 33 | 0.286 | 0.248 | 9 of 37 | 23 |

The interval column counts only ramps whose outcome is settled: finished, or still running past the interval's upper end.

**Read this as a weak result.** The curve does no better than the base rate at saying which unfinished ramps will finish, and at the later cutoffs many ramps finished before its 50% interval even began. Ramps are not drawn from one fixed distribution. Split by the year a ramp started, later starters have moved faster, and a pooled curve fitted on the early ones cannot see that:

| Ramp started | Ramps | Completed | First quartile (y) | Median (y) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1985-2009 | 28 | 21 | 10.1 | 14.5 |
| 2010-2014 | 36 | 20 | 9.1 | 11.7 |
| 2015-2019 | 40 | 11 | 7.3 | not reached |

So treat the pooled median as an order of magnitude -- around a decade, not the four years of the placeholder -- and as biased long for a technology starting now. It is not a calibrated forecast.
