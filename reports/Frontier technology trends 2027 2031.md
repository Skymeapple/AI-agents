# Agentic AI's power hunger reorders frontier technology

As of September 2026 the most consequential change across frontier science and technology is not any single breakthrough. It is that agentic AI is compounding faster than even recent forecasts assumed, and its demand for chips, memory, packaging and electricity has become the organising force for semiconductors, photonics, nuclear, geothermal and grid storage. METR's measured task horizon for frontier models has been doubling roughly every 4.3 months since 2023. One frontier lab's annualised revenue went from about $9B to more than $65B in seven months. The four largest US hyperscalers guided roughly $725-785B of 2026 capex, and WSTS now expects the chip market to reach about $1.51T in 2026, years ahead of the old "$1T by 2030" consensus. Outside the AI orbit, the picture splits. Electrons are winning: batteries, EVs and storage are growing 20-50% a year, and firm clean power is moving from permits to hardware. Molecules are in retreat: green hydrogen and green steel face cancellations and rescues, and durable carbon removal is almost entirely one buyer. Biotech is in a genuine registration wave, led by incretins now priced like mass-market drugs, the first in vivo CRISPR filing, and the first positive Phase 3 for a personalised mRNA cancer vaccine. Quantum computing reached real error-correction milestones, but its 2027-2031 urgency comes mainly from post-quantum migration deadlines rather than from any machine that can break encryption. Our most important and most urgent trends for 2027-2031 are long-horizon agents, the AI compute-power nexus, AI-enabled cyber offence, and the packaging and memory bottleneck. Every forecast probability in this report is our own `assumed` judgement, and several headline figures rest on search snippets or secondary sources, which we flag throughout.

## How to read the provenance grades

This report follows sciscout's rule that every number states where it came from. A figure tagged **`sourced`** comes from a named, cited source: a company filing, agency release, peer-reviewed paper or reputable press report. **`sourced·snippet`** means the researchers could see only a search-result summary of the cited page, because the network proxy blocked full fetches from metr.org, hai.stanford.edu, sec.gov, techcrunch.com, cnbc.com, thequantuminsider.com, wsts.org, cdr.fyi, rhg.com, weforum.org, technologyreview.com and others. **`sourced·secondary`** means the figure comes from an aggregator, blog or trade summary and not from the originating organisation. **`derived`** marks arithmetic we did on sourced inputs, such as growth rates or ratios. **`assumed`** marks judgement calls, and it covers **every forecast probability in this report without exception**. These probabilities are the research team's own estimates from the cited trend rates. They are not taken from Metaculus, any analyst or any company, and they have not been calibrated against a track record. The importance and urgency scores are also `assumed`, because we scored them by judgement from the notes and not through sciscout's citation-corpus pipeline.

## Measured AI capability and revenue are compounding faster than forecasts

The central measured fact of 2026 is acceleration, not plateau. METR's Time Horizon 1.1 update puts the post-2023 doubling time of the 50% task-completion horizon at **about 130.8 days (~4.3 months)**, against about 7 months over 2019-2025 `sourced·snippet` ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)). The frontier moved from about **2 h 17 min for GPT-5 (Aug 2025)** to about **12 h for Claude Opus 4.6 (Feb 2026)** `sourced·snippet` ([METR time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). When METR added an early Claude Mythos Preview in May 2026 it noted that "measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable with our current task suite." So the constraint on measuring progress is now the benchmark, not the models. METR itself warns that the metric depends on the task suite and has wide confidence intervals ([METR limitations note](https://metr.org/notes/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations/)). Static benchmarks are saturating. The 2026 AI Index reports SWE-bench going "from 60 to nearly 100 percent in a single year" `sourced·snippet` ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report)). The contamination-resistant SWE-bench Pro, by contrast, still tops out at **61.5% (public set, Sep 2026)** `sourced·secondary` ([Scale leaderboard](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro); [Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/swe-bench-pro)). At IMO 2026 a RedNote model reportedly scored a perfect 42/42, but official certification was not verified `sourced·secondary` ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3361482/worlds-first-ai-model-earn-perfect-score-maths-olympiad-comes-chinas-rednote)).

Money is following capability, and faster than capex. Anthropic's run-rate was reported at **more than $65B at end-July 2026, up from about $9B at end-2025** `sourced·snippet` ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/anthropics-annualized-revenue-surges-to-65b/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-revenue-run-rate-ipo-openai)). OpenAI's roughly $40B run-rate comes only from lower-quality secondary sources and should be treated as unconfirmed `sourced·secondary` ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/openai-revenue-2026-20b-arr-4b-month-path-to-profitability)). The hardest number in the AI economy is Nvidia's SEC filing: **Q2 FY2027 data-centre revenue of $89.0B, up 117% year on year** `sourced` ([Nvidia 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27pr.htm)). Hyperscaler 2026 capex guidance sums to **$725-785B, about +77%** `sourced·secondary` ([UncoverAlpha](https://www.uncoveralpha.com/p/amazon-google-microsoft-meta-q2-earnings); [TMT Finance](https://www.tmtfinance.com/intel/2026-hyperscaler-capex-tops-us700bn-analysis)). The IEA independently says big-tech capex topped $400B in 2025 and is expected to jump another 75% `sourced` ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/data-centre-electricity-use-surged-in-2025-even-with-tightening-bottlenecks-driving-a-scramble-for-solutions)). The strain is already visible. Alphabet reported negative quarterly free cash flow of about -$5.9B in Q2 2026, reportedly its first since the IPO `sourced·secondary`, not yet checked against the 10-Q. Price per unit of capability is collapsing: Epoch measures a **median ~50x per year fall** in the price of reaching a fixed capability, and about 200x per year for models released since 2024 `sourced` ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends)). Yet total spend rises, because agents consume far more tokens. This is a textbook Jevons dynamic and the main reason the power story below exists.

Adoption is broad but shallow. McKinsey's 2026 survey finds **23% of organisations scaling an agentic system somewhere but no more than 10% in any single function** `sourced` ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/the-state-of-ai-in-2026-on-the-road-to-roi.pdf)). Its Technology Trends Outlook adds that 89% of organisations use AI but only 37% report positive EBIT impact `sourced·snippet` ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-top-trends-in-tech)). The gap between frontier capability and enterprise absorption is the largest source of variance in 2027-2031 revenue forecasts.

AI for science has a clear validation hierarchy, and it is worth stating plainly because hype blurs it. Weather is **operational**: ECMWF's AIFS since February 2025, NOAA since January 2026 `sourced` ([CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/news/ai-weather-forecasting-goes-operational/); [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog/2026/farewell-external-ai-models)). Formal mathematics is **machine-verified but modest**: DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus solved **9 of 353 open Erdős problems** attempted and 44 of 492 OEIS conjectures, each Lean-checked, at "a few hundred dollars" per problem `sourced` ([arXiv 2605.22763](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)). AI-designed drugs are **clinical but unproven**. Insilico's rentosertib entered Phase 3 in IPF with 320 patients over 52 weeks after a Phase 2a gain of +98.4 mL FVC at 60 mg `sourced` ([Insilico](https://insilico.com/news/xmjsn4l091-insilico-initiates-phase-iii-clinical-tr)), and no AI-discovered drug has yet shown Phase 3 efficacy. Materials discovery remains **demonstration-grade and contested**: Nature corrected the Berkeley A-Lab paper in January 2026 after critics showed most "novel" compounds were already known `sourced·secondary` ([Cypris summary](https://www.cypris.ai/insights/ai-accelerated-materials-discovery-in-2025-how-generative-models-graph-neural-networks-and-autonomous-labs-are-transforming-r-d)), and a critical perspective found few GNoME predictions "credible, new and useful" `sourced` ([ACS Chem. Mater.](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643)).

The sharpest new risk is cyber offence. Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview "autonomously discovered thousands of previously unknown vulnerabilities across every major operating system and web browser". Access was restricted to about 40 organisations, and the US Treasury and Fed convened bank CEOs the day after the announcement `sourced·secondary` ([Cloud Security Alliance](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/ai-vuln-discovery-containment-claude-mythos-v1-0-csa-styled/); [Data Protection Report](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2026/05/when-ai-becomes-the-cyber-attacker-mythos-and-what-comes-next/)). Regulation is moving the other way. The EU's Digital Omnibus deferred Annex III high-risk obligations to **2 December 2027** and Annex I to **2 August 2028** `sourced` ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)). US federal preemption of state AI laws is unresolved after the Senate stripped a moratorium 99-1 ([TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/where-state-ai-legislation-stands-half-way-into-2026/)).

## Chips, packaging and electricity are now the binding constraints

The semiconductor market is being repriced by AI memory demand. SIA counted **$791.7B of 2025 sales (+25.6%)** `sourced` ([SIA](https://www.semiconductors.org/global-annual-semiconductor-sales-increase-25-6-to-791-7-billion-in-2025/)). WSTS's Spring 2026 forecast then made the largest upward revision on record: **2026 at ~$1.51T (+90%)**, with memory up ~250% to more than $800B, and 2027 at ~$1.9T `sourced·snippet`, via Digitimes because wsts.org was blocked ([WSTS](https://www.wsts.org/76/103/Global-Semiconductor-Market-Surges-Beyond-15T-2026); [Digitimes](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260605VL208/semiconductor-industry-wsts-growth-forecast-2026.html)). That is a price-driven number. Memory at more than $800B is roughly 3.6x its 2025 level `derived`, so a memory-cycle correction is the main way 2027 disappoints. TSMC's Q2 2026 revenue was **$40.2B at 67.7% gross margin**, full-year growth guidance was raised to above 40%, and capex is $60-64B `sourced` ([TSMC IR](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/quarterly-results/2026/q2)). All three leading foundries now run 2nm-class nodes in volume. Backside power arrives at TSMC with A16 in Q4 2026 `sourced·secondary` ([ETNews](https://en.etnews.com/20260819200004)). Intel has installed and accepted ASML's High-NA EXE:5200B for 14A, with risk production in 2027 ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/intel-installs-industrys-first-commercial-high-na-euv-lithography-tool-asml-twinscan-exe-5200b-sets-the-stage-for-14a)).

The real bottleneck sits between the chips. TSMC's CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity has grown about 80% a year, from ~35k wafers/month (end-2024) to a ~125-130k target (end-2026), and is still fully booked `sourced·secondary`: these are analyst estimates, not TSMC disclosures ([Troy Technical](https://troy-technical.com/2026/07/19/tsmc-achieves-record-q2-revenue-of-40-2b-with-67-7-gross-margin-cowos-fully-booked-through-2026-and-maps-14-reticle-packaging/)). **HBM4 entered mass production in February 2026** at SK hynix and Samsung `sourced` ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/01/28/news-sk-hynix-reportedly-to-supply-about-two-thirds-of-nvidia-hbm4-samsung-targets-early-delivery/)). Amazon attributed part of its capex raise to higher memory costs `sourced·secondary` ([UncoverAlpha](https://www.uncoveralpha.com/p/amazon-google-microsoft-meta-q2-earnings)). Photonics crossed into volume. TrendForce reports that Nvidia and Broadcom began volume ramps of **co-packaged optics switches in July 2026**, with optical-engine yield as the new constraint `sourced` ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260727-13151.html); [NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-spectrum-x-co-packaged-optics-networking-switches-ai-factories)). Export controls loosened at the margin. The January 2026 BIS rule moved H200-class licensing to case-by-case review with a 25% US revenue cut and a 50% volume cap `sourced` ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/15/2026-00789/revision-to-license-review-policy-for-advanced-computing-commodities)). Equipment controls keep SMIC on DUV multi-patterning. The widely repeated "5nm at 30-40% yield" figure for SMIC comes from a low-quality aggregator and should not be relied on `sourced·secondary`.

Electricity is the other wall. The IEA measured **17% growth in global data-centre electricity in 2025, and ~50% for AI-focused centres** `sourced` ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/data-centre-electricity-use-surged-in-2025-even-with-tightening-bottlenecks-driving-a-scramble-for-solutions)). It projects **~945 TWh by 2030**, more than double 2024 ([IEA Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary)). Epoch projects that the largest single training runs will need **4-16 GW by 2030** `sourced` ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/blog/power-demands-of-frontier-ai-training)). That demand is now visibly pulling firm clean power out of the permitting stage. The NRC issued its **first-ever construction permit for a commercial non-light-water reactor** (TerraPower Natrium, 4 March 2026) `sourced` ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/ne/articles/nrc-issues-construction-permit-terrapowers-natrium-advanced-reactor)). Meta signed nuclear agreements of up to **6.6 GW** `sourced` ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/meta-nuclear-energy-projects-power-american-ai-leadership/)). Palisades began loading fuel on 30 August 2026, the first US restart of a retired plant, but has already slipped once and gives no restart date `sourced` ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-07-08/article-8187/palisades-restart-projects-holtec-ipo-lawsuit-dismissal-but-no-restart-date/)). **Fervo's Cape Station reached first power on 24 September 2026**, the first utility-scale enhanced geothermal plant anywhere to sync to a grid, with ~500 MW planned by 2028 `sourced`, company release ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/24/3368115/0/en/fervo-energy-achieves-first-power-at-cape-station-a-landmark-moment-for-the-future-of-enhanced-geothermal-systems.html)). Carnegie cautions that hyperscaler nuclear commitments will not translate one-for-one into supply ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/beyond-the-hype-assessing-hyperscaler-nuclear-commitments-against-us-energy-realities)).

Storage is the fastest-scaling piece of the energy system. BNEF's December 2025 survey put average lithium-ion packs at **$108/kWh, LFP at $81/kWh and stationary storage at $70/kWh**, down 45% in a year for stationary `sourced` ([BNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/); [ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/2025/12/09/bnef-lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-kwh-stationary-storage-becomes-lowest-price-segment/)). Global storage additions were **112 GW/307 GWh in 2025 (+48%)**, and BNEF forecasts 158 GW/459 GWh for 2026 `sourced` ([Energy-Storage.News](https://www.energy-storage.news/bloombergnef-forecasts-158gw-of-global-energy-storage-deployments-in-2026/)). Sodium-ion reached GWh-scale deployment through CATL's Naxtra line `sourced`, company ([CATL](https://www.catl.com/en/news/6720.html)). True all-solid-state batteries remain at the pilot-line stage. The first mass-produced "semi-solid" car, SAIC's MG4 Anxin, still carries ~5% liquid electrolyte ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2025/12/18/mg-delivers-first-mass-produced-ev-with-semi-solid-state-battery/)). Solar's frontier is a certified **35.5% perovskite-silicon tandem cell** (LONGi, July 2026) `sourced` ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/07/15/longi-sets-new-world-record-with-35-5-efficient-perovskite-silicon-tandem-cell/)). Commercial modules trail at ~26.9% `sourced·secondary`, and durability is still the bankability question.

Fusion shows real physics and record capital, but its dates remain claims. NIF's April 2025 shot produced **8.6 MJ at a target gain of 4.13**. Wall-plug input is roughly 100 times higher, so there is no net electrical gain `sourced` ([LLNL](https://lasers.llnl.gov/about/keys-to-success/nif-sets-power-energy-records)). Private fusion raised a record **$4.48B in the year to July 2026**, bringing the cumulative total to $14.24B `sourced` ([FIA](https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/fusion-industry-attracts-record-annual-funding-of-4-48bn-raising-total-to-14-24bn/)). Helion holds what it calls the first fusion power-plant licences and a promise of 50 MW to Microsoft by 2028, but it has no peer-reviewed electricity demonstration ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/helion-hits-15-5b-valuation-with-465m-in-new-cash-to-commercialize-fusion-this-decade/)). CFS's SPARC first-plasma date conflicts across sources: 2026 per CFS's long-standing target, 2027 per some secondary reporting ([The Fusion Report](https://thefusionreport.com/commonwealth-fusion-systems-a-hot-start-for-2026/)). ITER will not start research operations until 2034 ([Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/iter-fusion-reactor-hit-by-massive-decade-long-delay-and-e5bn-price-hike/)).

## Quantum's real deadline is cryptographic, not computational

Quantum error correction has moved from one below-threshold memory to dozens of logical qubits. After Google Willow's below-threshold surface code (Λ≈2.14, December 2024) `sourced` ([Google Quantum AI](https://quantumai.google/qecmilestone)), Quantinuum's Helios launched with **48-50 logical qubits at ~2:1 physical overhead** `sourced` ([Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quantinuum-launches-helios-quantum-computer-with-industry-leading-fidelity-and-singapore-partnership/)). Harvard/QuEra showed up to 96 logical qubits on 448 atoms `sourced·secondary` ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-computing-companies/quera/)). IBM decoded qLDPC codes in real time in under 480 ns ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-11-12-ibm-delivers-new-quantum-processors,-software,-and-algorithm-breakthroughs-on-path-to-advantage-and-fault-tolerance)). These headline counts are **not comparable across vendors**. A 2:1 overhead implies error-detecting or low-distance codes, while Google's is a distance-7 surface code. The field has no public ~100-logical-qubit machine running deep circuits at 10⁻⁶ logical error. Roadmaps converge on **2029** for the first fault-tolerant systems: IBM Starling with 200 logical qubits and 10⁸ gates, Quantinuum Apollo, and Microsoft's Majorana target ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-06-10-IBM-Sets-the-Course-to-Build-Worlds-First-Large-Scale,-Fault-Tolerant-Quantum-Computer-at-New-IBM-Quantum-Data-Center); [Quantinuum S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002110105/000162828026032836/quantinuum-sx1.htm)). Microsoft's topological claims remain contested. DARPA began independent testing only on 22 September 2026 `sourced·snippet` ([TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/22/microsoft-gives-darpa-access-to-majorana-system-opens-maryland-quantum-research-center/)). Capital ran ahead of revenue. McKinsey counts **$12.6B invested in 2025 (6.3x)** against a little over $1B of vendor revenue `sourced` ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-technology/our-insights/mckinsey-quantum-technology-monitor-2026-a-commercial-tipping-point)), and 2026 brought a listing wave that included Quantinuum's IPO ([SEC 424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm)).

The number that moved most is the estimated cost of breaking RSA-2048. Estimates fell from ~20M physical qubits (2019) to **under 1M (Gidney, May 2025)**, then to **~100k with qLDPC "Pinnacle" (Feb 2026)** and ~10k neutral atoms at long runtimes (Mar 2026). All of these are `sourced` estimates for unbuilt architectures, and the 2026 ones are not directly comparable to Gidney's ([Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/shor-qldpc-codes-and-the-compression-of-rsa-2048-resource-estimates-part-i/); [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/02/13/new-architecture-could-cut-quantum-hardware-needed-to-break-rsa-2048-by-tenfold-study-finds/)). Expert opinion moved in step. GRI's survey puts a cryptographically relevant quantum computer within 10 years at 28-49%, the largest jump in its seven-year history `sourced` ([GRI](https://globalriskinstitute.org/publication/quantum-threat-timeline-report-2025b/)). NIST IR 8547 deprecates RSA-2048 and P-256 after 2030 and disallows RSA/ECC after 2035 ([NIST](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2024/NIST.IR.8547.ipd.pdf)). New US national-security acquisitions must be CNSA 2.0-compliant from 1 January 2027 `sourced·secondary`. We put a working RSA-2048 break by end-2031 at only **5-10%** `assumed`, but migration deadlines bite regardless of whether that machine arrives. That is why post-quantum migration scores more urgent than quantum computing itself.

## Biotech enters a registration wave while incretin prices collapse

Incretins are now the largest drug franchise in the world, and their economics are turning from scarcity to volume. Lilly's Mounjaro plus Zepbound earned **$14.9B in Q2 2026 alone**, and Lilly raised its 2026 revenue guidance to **$85-87B** `sourced·snippet` ([Lilly](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-raises-full); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/eli-lilly-lly-earnings-q2-2026.html)). Oral drugs have arrived: the Wegovy pill was approved in December 2025 and made DKK 3.1B in Q2, and Lilly's orforglipron (Foundayo) was approved in April 2026 ([Novo](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/04/3338662/0/en/Novo-Nordisk-reports-adjusted-operating-profit-of-DKK-33-389-million-for-Q2-2026-and-raises-full-year-outlook.html)). Prices are falling on several fronts at once. The Medicare MFN price is **$245/month** ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/trump-announces-deals-with-eli-lilly-novo-nordisk-for-lower-weight-loss-drug-prices)). TrumpRx lists the Wegovy pill from $149. Novo cuts the Wegovy list price to **$675 from 1 January 2027** `sourced·snippet` ([Novo 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000353278/000035327826000023/caq22026.htm)). Semaglutide generics launched in India and Canada in 2026. The next generation raises efficacy again: retatrutide reached **25.0% mean weight loss at 80 weeks** in TRIUMPH-1 `sourced` ([Lilly](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss)). Gene editing crossed from proof of concept to filing. Intellia's in vivo CRISPR therapy lonvo-z left **62% of hereditary angioedema patients attack-free versus 11% on placebo**. Lilly/Verve's one-shot PCSK9 base editor cut LDL-C by up to 62% in NEJM `sourced` ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2601283)). The FDA's draft "plausible mechanism" guidance (February 2026) opens a path for bespoke editors after baby KJ ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/23/nx-s1-5720948/fda-rare-disease-gene-therapy)). Ex vivo Casgevy is growing fast from a small base: **$76M in Q2 2026, +151% YoY** `sourced·snippet` ([Vertex](https://news.vrtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/vertex-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)).

The disagreements between the axes carry the most information. **Fusion** is the starkest case of importance without urgency. If it works it changes the energy system, but no grid electricity is plausible before 2028 even on company claims, and ITER's schedule runs to 2039. For a 2027-2031 decision, it is a watch item and not a race. **Quantum error correction** is similar. What makes quantum urgent is **PQC migration**, whose deadlines (CNSA 2.0 acquisitions from 2027, NIST deprecation after 2030) arrive regardless of hardware progress. So we split the two, and the split explains why PQC outranks the computers themselves. **AI for science** has the reverse gap to humanoids: it is highly important because it feeds every other field on this list, but only weather is operational, and the payoffs in drugs and materials run through 5-10 year trial and validation cycles, so urgency is moderate. **Humanoids** are urgent without matching importance. Capital, Chinese subsidies and $5-40B valuations make it a crowded race, but verified productive deployment is thin and shipments are tiny next to 542,000 industrial-robot installs a year. **Drones** and **rare earths** score urgent because of hard external clocks, a live war and the 10 November 2026 control expiry, more than because of long-run breadth. **Green hydrogen and CDR** score low on urgency for a negative reason: the market is decelerating, so the question for 2027-2031 is survival of niches, not a race to scale.

## Cross-field interactions set the pace more than any single field

Five couplings determine how these trends play out together. **First, AI demand is the pacing load for energy and semiconductors.** A ~50-200x per year fall in cost per capability does not cut spending, because agents consume far more tokens. That drives hyperscaler capex, which drives the WSTS memory supercycle and CoWoS/HBM scarcity. That in turn drives data-centre electricity toward the IEA's ~945 TWh, and that is the commercial reason Natrium, Palisades, Crane, Fervo and Helion have hyperscaler buyers. A slowdown in AI revenue growth would therefore propagate within a few quarters into memory prices, packaging utilisation, and the financing appetite for first-of-a-kind reactors and geothermal. That is the single largest correlated risk across this report. **Second, AI capability couples to security and quantum.** Mythos-class vulnerability discovery raises the cost of slow patching today. Falling RSA resource estimates and the PQC deadlines raise the cost of slow cryptographic migration tomorrow. Both push the same organisations to inventory and modernise their systems in the same 2027-2030 window. **Third, AI for science feeds biotech and materials, but validation, not generation, is the bottleneck.** Rentosertib's Phase 3, Isomorphic's first-in-human trial slipping to end-2026 ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/isomorphic-labs-alphafold-ai-drug-discovery-trials)) and the A-Lab correction all show that the rate-limiting step is wet-lab and clinical confirmation. Autonomous-lab maturity is therefore the hidden variable behind AI-for-science payoffs. **Fourth, batteries link energy, vehicles, robotics and defence.** $70/kWh stationary packs make storage the fastest-growing grid asset. The same cell supply chain powers EVs, humanoids and FPV drones. That supply chain is Chinese-dominated and exposed to rare-earth and critical-mineral controls. **Fifth, capital concentration couples everything.** With 86% of US VC going to AI, non-AI deep tech in climate, materials and quantum survives mainly where it attaches to AI infrastructure through power, cooling, photonics or minerals. That is why geothermal and nuclear attract hyperscaler money while green steel needs a rescue.

## What would change these forecasts

The largest single swing factor is **AI revenue relative to capex**. If lab revenue keeps compounding faster than hyperscaler spending, as it did in 2026 (roughly 5-7x against ~1.8x `derived`), the compute, semiconductor, data-centre-power and firm-power forecasts should all move up together. A visible revenue stall, a failed or delayed Anthropic IPO, or a second quarter of negative Alphabet free cash flow with capex cuts should move them all down together, with memory prices falling first. Because these forecasts are correlated, readers should not treat them as independent bets. **A new METR task suite** would either confirm the ~4-month doubling above 16 h or reveal a slowdown toward 7 months. That single measurement moves the horizon forecasts by 15-25 points `assumed`. **Open-weight models matching Mythos-class cyber capability** would raise the $1B-incident probability sharply and would likely reverse the regulatory drift toward delay. On quantum, a **Google distance-9/11 result or a logical-gate error rate near 10⁻⁶** would pull the fault-tolerance and CRQC estimates forward. Independent DARPA confirmation of topological protection would add a modality. Another failed advantage claim would push the useful-advantage forecast back. In energy, **Palisades' startup** is the test of whether restarts can hit dates. If it slips past mid-2027, the Crane and SMR forecasts should fall. Fervo's first-year capacity factor decides whether EGS is a technology or a single project. For biotech, **the lonvo-z label and any liver-safety language** set the template for the whole in vivo editing class. The rentosertib enrolment pace is the first real test of whether AI-discovered molecules behave differently in late-stage trials. In physical AI, a **Waymo quarter clearly above 600-700k rides/week** would restore the 1M/week path. A verified 1,000-unit productive humanoid deployment would justify raising humanoid importance, not just urgency. On the geopolitical side, **China's 10 November 2026 rare-earth decision** and US export-licence volumes for H200-class chips can each shift semiconductor, robotics and EV supply forecasts within one quarter.

## Gaps and conflicting sources

The evidence has three structural weaknesses that readers should weigh. First, **many headline numbers are snippet-level**. METR horizons, the AI Index figures, Anthropic's run-rate, WSTS's $1.51T, Lilly and Novo quarterly detail, CDR.fyi tonnages, Rhodium's investment totals and the humanoid shipment tracker were all seen through search summaries because the proxy blocked the primary pages. They are probably right but have not been verified at the source. Second, **some sources conflict outright**, and we did not resolve every conflict:

- **Rentosertib Phase 3:** the start was reported in July by one source and September by another. The readout is "late 2027 at the earliest" in one set of notes and 2028-2029 in another. A 52-week trial that began enrolling in mid-2026 makes 2028 the realistic readout.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing for 2027-2031 is that frontier technology is no longer a set of parallel races. It is one demand shock, from AI, propagating through a chain of physical bottlenecks: memory, packaging, photonics, electricity, firm power, minerals. Each field's near-term trajectory depends on where it sits in that chain. Fields that sell into the chain, such as nuclear restarts, geothermal, storage and co-packaged optics, gained years of commercial runway in 2026. Fields outside it, such as green hydrogen, green steel and most carbon removal, lost capital and momentum even where their science held up. That makes the ratio of AI revenue to AI capex the most important cross-field leading indicator in this report. It is more predictive for energy and semiconductor forecasts than any energy or semiconductor metric.

The second insight is about evidence. The trends that score most urgent are also the ones whose key measurements are breaking down. METR cannot measure above ~16 hours, SWE-bench Verified is saturated, cross-vendor logical-qubit counts are incomparable, and humanoid shipment trackers disagree by 30%. The fields with the cleanest evidence are regulated or audited: FDA dockets, NRC permits, SEC filings, BNEF surveys and Waymo's safety hub. Their forecasts deserve more confidence than their lower urgency scores suggest. A reader using these probabilities should therefore weight them by evidence grade, not just by priority score. That is the discipline sciscout's provenance grades exist to enforce.
