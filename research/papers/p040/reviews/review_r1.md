# Review (Round 1): "Who Writes the Constitution? An Authorship Audit of Public-Input AI Constitutions, with Evidence from Collective Constitutional AI"

Reviewer role: area chair–level independent reviewer. Target venue: FAccT 2027.

## 1. Summary

The paper takes the normative question "who should write AI constitutions/values?" and narrows it to a descriptive, measurable one: in a deployed public-input process, how much of the resulting constitution is fixed by the participants and how much by the designer? It proposes a four-channel "authorship audit":
- agenda: seed share, author concentration, exposure;
- sample: bootstrap sample-swap Jaccard distance;
- designer: multiverse designer-swap distance plus a two-way variance decomposition;
- pipeline integrity: re-running released code and testing its intermediate outputs against a null.

It gives three short propositions:
- P1, partition leverage on minimum-group agreement;
- P2, τ-consensus filters cannot guarantee JR;
- P3, a consensus core completed by greedy coverage satisfies JR under a condition on the uncovered set.

It applies the audit to the public CCAI Polis data (1,002 × 275, 34,563 votes) and to 16 openData Polis conversations. The main empirical claims are:
- the 72 "consensus in both groups" pipelines give quite different 58-statement sets (mean Jaccard 0.57);
- bootstrap resampling at CCAI's scale moves the set even more;
- across conversations, the designer/sample ratio rises with n (ρ = 0.95);
- count-based proportional rules on sparse Polis data over-select organizer seeds (31–35% vs 7.6% of the pool);
- the released CCAI opinion-group labels are misaligned with participants because of a sort/positional-assignment bug in the released notebook.

## 2. Verification performed

**Code rerun.** I copied the code to a scratch directory and reran `run_ccai.py` (B=40, BS=25), `run_ccai_addendum.py` and `run_opendata.py` (B=20) with single-threaded BLAS. The run used scikit-learn 1.9.1.

What reproduces:
- Nearly every number in Sections 6.1, 6.3, 6.4, 6.5 and Table 1 reproduces to the reported precision. This includes: 34,563 votes; 12.5% density; 78.9/13.2/7.9% agree/disagree/pass; 61 organizer statements and 21 seeds; 156 authors; 28.5% top-decile share; seen 386 vs 104; Spearman −0.83; 96–220 admitted statements at τ = 0.72; union 233 and intersection 56; mean consensus Jaccard 0.569 (min 0.275); 10 always-in and 136 ever-in; 0.545/0.105, 7 and 162 over all 79 designs; 210/65/145 for P1; every row of Table 1; the sample-size curve 0.729→0.565 and 0.556→0.441; the 12.9/35.5/51.6% decomposition; seed shares 31.0–34.5% and 27.6%; exposure correlations 0.80/0.30; bootstrap-to-full Jaccard 0.85/0.73/0.82; group-gap ranges; and the curve fits.
- `ccai_design_audit.csv` is byte-identical to the original.
- `opendata_decomposition.csv` reproduces exactly (maximum absolute difference 0).

**Mismatch: re-aligned-label results are not deterministic.** All numbers that depend on the "re-aligned" (corrected) labels changed on rerun. The cause is that `PCA(2).fit(Xs.T)` in `run_ccai.py` and `run_ccai_addendum.py` uses scikit-learn's `auto` solver. On this 275 × 1002 matrix that selects the *randomized* SVD solver with no `random_state`. Repeated calls flip between two solutions.

| Quantity | Paper | My rerun / repeated calls |
|---|---|---|
| Re-aligned τ = 0.72 set size | 176 | 181 or 177 |
| Statements that drop out / enter | 19 / 7 | 15 / 8 or 17 / 6 |
| Changes out of union ("26 of 195", in the abstract) | 26 of 195 | 23 of 196 or 23 of 194 |
| Released vs re-aligned Jaccard | 0.867 | 0.883 |
| Top-58 Jaccard | 0.73 | 0.76 |
| Re-aligned vs overall-majority Jaccard | 0.838 | 0.862 |
| Re-aligned separation | 0.1065 | 0.1048 |
| Positional-replica ARI | 0.83–0.94 | 0.83–0.96 |

The qualitative finding survives every draw. The positional-replica ARI is always ≥ 0.83, the correctly aligned ARI is always ≈ −0.004, and the re-aligned separation is always about 10 SD above the null. But a number quoted in the abstract cannot be regenerated by the released code. Under this review's protocol that is a fatal reproducibility defect, although it is trivial to fix: seed the PCA or use `svd_solver='full'`, and report the range across seeds.

**Statistics not produced by released code.** `results/opendata_summary.txt` is not produced by any released script. This file holds the Spearman ρ = 0.95, both Wilcoxon tests, the OLS t-values and the median variance shares. I recomputed these from `opendata_decomposition.csv` and they match: ρ = 0.95, p = 1.8e-8; full-multiverse Wilcoxon p = 0.0076; OLS t = 3.30 for log n and 1.25 for log M. The analysis script must still be released.

**Notebook check.** I read `ccai_data_processing.ipynb`:
- Cell 10 sorts `vals` by participant.
- PCA and k-means run on the sorted matrix.
- Cell 31 assigns labels positionally with `cleaned_votes_df['group-id'] = [...]` to the *unsorted* frame.

So the claimed bug is really present in the released notebook. The limitation that "the released notebook truncates part of the cluster-assignment step" does not match what I see: the assignment is complete. The notebook does, however, choose K by silhouette score, while the replica fixes K = 2.

**Extra robustness checks I ran.**
- The n–ratio association survives dropping the five non-independent Austrian conversations (ρ = 0.86, p = 6e-4).
- It also survives adding log(k/M) as a covariate (t for log n = 3.56) and controlling for log density (t = 4.4).
- Neither swap distance alone correlates with n (ρ = −0.09 for sample-swap and 0.39 for designer-swap, both n.s.).
- In the consensus-only family, designer-swap is *not* larger than sample-swap (Wilcoxon p = 0.94). The paper reports only the full-multiverse test.

## 4. Novelty assessment

- **CCAI reanalysis.** I found no prior independent reanalysis of the CCAI Polis data. The pipeline-integrity finding is, as far as I can tell, new and useful.
- **The "authorship channel" framing is close to existing work that is not cited.** Gelauff (arXiv:2608.28368, Aug 2026), "It Takes Three to Converse: … how the Developer, the Convener and the Participant shaped 119 Polis conversations", argues that Polis outputs are co-shaped by developer, convener and participant. It also notes that the exported clustering cannot be reproduced from the votes alone. This should be discussed.
- **Partition dependence of Polis's group-aware consensus has been studied.** Blair, de Raaij, Procaccia, Rubin-Toles & Shah, "The Structure of Bridging" (2026), propose bridging metrics that do not depend on a single partition and that hold up under missing votes. That work speaks directly to P1 and to the "designer chooses the partition" channel.
- **P3's hybrid is close to existing work.** Revel et al. (ICML 2025, arXiv:2503.18962), "Representative Ranking for Deliberation in the Public Sphere", maximize a bridging or other score subject to a JR constraint, and test this in a deployment.
- **The theory is elementary.**
  - P1 is a two-line counting argument.
  - The second part of P2 is immediate.
  - P3 reduces, at k₀ = 0, to the standard fact that greedy CC satisfies JR, and the proof is the usual one.
  - That threshold or consensus filters can fail JR is folklore in ABC voting.

  These results are correct, but they are framing devices rather than contributions.
- **Overall.** The empirical audit is a moderately novel and careful application of multiverse analysis to a governance pipeline. The theory is incremental.

## 5. Proof check

- **P1.**
  - (i) and (ii) are correct.
  - (iii) is correct when (1−p)N ≤ ⌈αN⌉.
  - Corollary arithmetic: 1 − 0.382 × 0.28 = 0.893, correct.
  - The bound assumes full information. In CCAI each statement is seen by about 100 voters, so the relevant α is the minority share *among that statement's viewers*, which varies by statement.
  - The partition must be common to all statements and in practice is k-means on a 2-D PCA. The paper acknowledges this ("exposure, not effect"), but then reports "145 of 210 statements are partition-vulnerable" as a headline. That number mainly restates how close most agreement rates are to 0.72–0.89.
- **P2.** The construction is correct. I checked |F| ≥ (1−τ)N − g ≥ N/k under the stated N condition, admissibility of C, and inadmissibility of c_F using τ > 1/2.
  - Conceptual weakness: the partition is fixed *before* the adversarial profile. In Polis the partition is endogenous, and on the constructed profile k-means would isolate F as its own group. The second clause then applies, but only when F is itself a group.
  - The theorem says nothing about how often violations occur, so "matches Proposition 2's prediction" (Sec. 6.5) is an over-reading. Approval voting (AV) fails JR in exactly the same 5 conversations, which suggests the violations come from any non-proportional rule, not from consensus filtering specifically.
- **P3.** Correct. The newly covered sets are disjoint subsets of U \ X.
  - On CCAI the condition is vacuous (|U| = 15 vs a bound of 311).
  - Empirically, the hybrid does *not* keep bridging properties. Its group gap is 0.17, like the proportional rules, and its seed share is 27.6%. This undercuts the claim that it "keeps bridging and guarantees JR."

## 6. Strengths

1. A sharp, operational recasting of a normative question, with a sensible decomposition into channels.
2. An exceptionally transparent empirical pipeline. Almost every reported number reproduces exactly from public data with modest compute.
3. A real, verifiable pipeline-integrity finding in a high-profile dataset, with an appropriate null test and appropriately hedged conclusions.
4. Honest limitations. The paper acknowledges that its unit of analysis is statements rather than principles, the bootstrap caveats, and the small number of conversations.
5. The exposure-capture result is a practically useful warning for anyone running proportional rules on Polis exports.
6. Single-knob analysis (Table 1) lets readers reweight the multiverse.

## 7. Weaknesses

**Fatal (protocol-level, easily fixed)**
- F1. The re-aligned-label results, including "26 of 195" in the abstract and Sec. 6.2, are not reproducible because of unseeded randomized PCA. A rerun gives 23 of 196 or 23 of 194, a Jaccard of 0.88 rather than 0.867, and a top-58 Jaccard of 0.76 rather than 0.73.
- F2. The cross-conversation statistics (ρ = 0.95, Wilcoxon, OLS, median variance shares) come from `opendata_summary.txt`, which no released script produces. They are correct when recomputed, but the claim is not backed by released code.

**Major**
- M1. **Designer-vs-sample comparability depends on the multiverse.** Designer-swap depends entirely on which and how many designs are admitted. Adding near-duplicate designs lowers it, and adding rule families raises it. "Designer dominates at n ≳ 900" therefore holds relative to the authors' multiverse only. In the consensus family that matches CCAI's published description, designer-swap is not larger than sample-swap overall (p = 0.94). The ratios in the "dominant" conversations are 1.03–1.14 and come with no confidence intervals (B = 20). The abstract's "designer choices dominate" is too strong. The paper needs bootstrap CIs on swap distances and ratios, and a sensitivity analysis over multiverse composition.
- M2. **Sample-swap mixes in algorithmic noise.** The k-means `random_state` is set to the bootstrap index. Sample-swap, which compares across b, therefore includes clustering-initialization noise, while designer-swap, which is computed within b, does not. Bootstrap duplicates can also distort k-means. Both effects inflate the sample channel. The fix is to separate the seed from b, or to average over seeds.
- M3. **The scale "regularity" is weakly interpretable.**
  - The ratio's range is small (0.85–1.14).
  - Neither component alone tracks n.
  - n is confounded with M, density and k/M; k is set mechanically by clip(0.2M).
  - 5 of 16 conversations come from one Austrian process.

  My checks show the association survives several controls, which is to the paper's credit. But "designer influence overtakes sampling influence as processes grow" is a claim about a ±15% ratio change in a corpus of 16 conversations, with CCAI itself, at n = 1,002 and ratio 0.78, as a counterexample that the paper explains post hoc. The falsifiable prediction P1 then carves out an exposure condition (≥ 150 votes per statement) after seeing CCAI.
- M4. **Theory is thin relative to how it is framed.** See Section 5:
  - P2 assumes an exogenous partition, and its empirical "confirmation" is not specific to consensus filtering (AV fails identically).
  - P3's guarantee is vacuous on CCAI.
  - The hybrid gives up bridging properties in practice.
- M5. **The proportional-rule finding is partly a strawman.** Running PAV, MES or CC on raw approvals with unseen treated as disapproval is a choice that the cited literature (Halpern et al.) already warns against. The "agenda capture" result is mostly an exposure artefact that the paper's own design produces. It is valuable as a caution, but it should not be presented as a property of proportional rules. An exposure-normalized variant (approval rate, or inverse-propensity weighting) should be in the multiverse, and P2-exposure should be tested on it.
- M6. **Missing closely related work** (Section 4): Gelauff 2026, Blair et al. 2026, Revel et al. 2025. All three bear directly on the novelty claims for the channel framing, partition dependence and the hybrid.
- M7. **The gap between the unit of analysis and the normative conclusion is large.** The audit measures pre-curation statement sets. The final CCAI constitution was produced by manual merging that is not in the release. The conclusion that "the designer's share of authorship should be reported" is reasonable, but the quantitative shares (30–56%) do not transfer to the constitution that was actually used.

**Minor**
- m1. "Bootstrap-to-full about 0.59 for rate-based rules": `av|decided` is 0.495 and the consensus mean is 0.536.
- m2. "Designer would overtake ... only at tens of thousands of participants": the consensus-family fit never crosses over (crossover = None). Only the full multiverse crosses, at about 60k. Say so.
- m3. The limitation that the notebook "truncates part of the cluster-assignment step" appears inaccurate. Also mention that the notebook selects K by silhouette score.
- m4. Small et al. 2021 is in the references but never cited.
- m5. The notebook's group-consensus function would raise an error rather than return 0 when no group member agrees. This is worth one line in Channel D.
- m6. The threshold rule uses strict ">" (as the notebook does), while the theory section uses "≥". Harmonize.
- m7. Report B and the uncertainty (SE or CI) next to every swap distance and every variance share.

## 8. Questions for the authors

1. With PCA seeded, what is the distribution of "changes out of the union" and of the top-58 Jaccard across, say, 50 PCA and k-means seeds?
2. How do the designer/sample ratios and their CIs change if the k-means seed is decoupled from the bootstrap index?
3. Does the n-trend survive when k is fixed as a fraction of M, or fixed at an absolute value?
4. If proportional rules use exposure-normalized approvals, does the seed over-representation disappear, as P2-exposure predicts?
5. Could you contact the CCAI authors to confirm the misalignment, and say whether the published constitution was derived from `clean_comments.csv` or from the Polis report's own group statistics?
6. How do you weight designs? Would a principled prior over designs (for example, from a survey of practitioners) change the conclusions?

## 9. Does the conclusion follow from the evidence?

Partly.
- **Follows:** "authorship is measurable", "designer choices are non-trivial", "sampling noise at CCAI's exposure levels is large", and "the released labels look misaligned".
- **Does not yet follow:** "designer dominates at scale". It rests on small ratio differences, no CIs, and a multiverse the authors chose; the consensus-family test is null.
- **Over-read:** "P2/P3 are borne out on real data".

The sub-question is sharp and well posed, but the paper's answer to the original normative question is only indirect.

## 11. Decision

**Major revision.** The empirical work is careful and almost entirely reproducible, and the CCAI integrity finding is a real contribution. Before acceptance the paper needs to:
- make the corrected-label analysis deterministic;
- release the cross-conversation analysis script;
- add uncertainty quantification;
- run a multiverse-sensitivity analysis on the designer-vs-sample comparison;
- decouple clustering seeds from bootstrap draws;
- engage with the closely related 2025–2026 work;
- tone down the scale and theory claims.
